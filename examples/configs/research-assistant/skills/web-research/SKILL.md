# Web research

A repeatable loop for turning a question into a well-sourced answer.

## 1. Frame the question

- Restate the question in one sentence and list the concrete sub-questions that,
  once answered, fully address it.
- Decide what kinds of sources count as authoritative for this topic (papers,
  standards, official docs, reputable reporting).

## 2. Gather

- Collect several independent sources per sub-question. Favor primary sources
  over commentary, and recent material over stale material.
- For each source, capture: title, author/publisher, date, URL, and the
  specific claim you are taking from it.

## 3. Vet

- Check who published it and when, whether it is primary or secondary, and
  whether other independent sources corroborate the claim.
- Discard or down-weight sources you cannot verify; note the gap explicitly.

## 4. Synthesize and cite

- Compare sources, reconcile or surface disagreements, and separate established
  facts from open questions.
- Cite every non-obvious claim. Use `format_citation` for consistent references
  and collect them in a references section.

## Quality bar

- Every factual claim is traceable to a cited source.
- Confidence and gaps are stated plainly.
- The answer leads with the conclusion, then the evidence.
